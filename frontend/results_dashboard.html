Why we are not recommending the best strategy yet

We tested many versions of each idea. When you try enough versions, one of them will look good by pure chance — like flipping 30 coins and calling the luckiest one "skilled". This test asks: after allowing for the number of tries, how sure can we be that the winner's lead is real? Science usually asks for 95%.

What we recommend

    Words used on this page

    Yearly return (CAGR)
    The average growth per year, compounded. 20% means 100 becomes 120 in a typical year.
    Risk-adjusted score (Sharpe)
    Return divided by how bumpy the ride was. Higher is better; above 1 is good, below 0.5 is weak.
    Worst fall (max drawdown)
    The biggest drop from a high point to a later low. −45% means 100 fell to 55 at some point.
    Equal-weight basket
    Owning all the same stocks in equal amounts and doing nothing clever. The fair yardstick, because it includes dividends and costs like the strategies do.
    KSE-100
    The index itself. It excludes dividends (often 5–8% a year on the PSX), so it understates what an investor actually earned.
    Holdout
    The last two years (Nov 2024–Nov 2026), kept locked away and looked at only once at the very end, as a final honest test.
    Costs
    Every trade pays about 0.3% each way (commission, tax, fees, price slippage). Trading every day adds up fast.
    Luck test (deflated Sharpe)
    How confident we can be that a strategy's lead is real skill and not the luckiest of many tries.
